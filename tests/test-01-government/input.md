## 2. Research Interview Record

**Consultant A:** Director Zhang, could you describe the current situation of your citizen service hall operations?

**Director Zhang:** Our service hall handles around 2,800 citizen visits per day across 46 service windows. We cover more than 310 government service items, from business registration to social security matters. The biggest issue is that citizens often need to visit multiple windows for a single matter. For example, opening a small restaurant requires visits to 5 different windows across 3 departments, and the citizen must fill in largely the same information 5 times. We receive complaints about this almost daily.

**Consultant A:** Section Chief Liu, from an operations perspective, what are the main bottlenecks?

**Section Chief Liu:** Three things. First, our staff spend roughly 40% of their working time re-entering data that already exists in another department's system, because the systems do not talk to each other. Second, peak-hour queues regularly exceed 90 minutes, and our satisfaction surveys show waiting time is the number one complaint, mentioned in 63% of negative feedback. Third, our approval processes are still largely paper-driven. A standard business permit involves 11 paper forms, 4 physical chops, and internal courier transfer between two office buildings. End-to-end approval time averages 13 working days, while the provincial target is 5.

**Consultant A:** Engineer Chen, what does the current system landscape look like?

**Engineer Chen:** We operate 17 separate business systems built over the past decade by 9 different vendors. There is no unified data standard. Citizen identity data exists in at least 6 systems with inconsistent formats. We attempted a data-sharing project two years ago but it stalled because there was no unified platform, only point-to-point interfaces, which became unmaintainable after about 20 connections. We also have almost no online service capability — only 38 of our 310 service items can be initiated online, and even those require an in-person visit to complete.

**Consultant A:** What about staffing and skills?

**Section Chief Liu:** Frontline staff turnover is high, around 25% annually for contract staff. Training a new window clerk to handle the full item catalog takes nearly 3 months because knowledge lives in scattered documents and in the heads of senior staff. Service quality varies a lot between experienced and new staff.

**Consultant A:** Director Zhang, what outcomes would leadership consider a success?

**Director Zhang:** The city government has set clear targets: 80% of service items available fully online within two years, average hall waiting time under 30 minutes, one-form submission for all composite matters, and approval time cut to under 5 working days for standard items. We also face annual provincial digital-government assessments, and our current ranking of 14th out of 21 cities in the province is considered unacceptable by city leadership. There is real political pressure to move up into the top 8 within two assessment cycles.

## 3. Customer Key Pain Points

1. Citizens must visit multiple windows and repeatedly submit identical information for a single composite matter (5 windows / 3 departments for common scenarios).
2. 17 siloed business systems from 9 vendors with no unified data standard; citizen identity data duplicated inconsistently across at least 6 systems.
3. Peak-hour waiting time exceeds 90 minutes; waiting time is cited in 63% of negative satisfaction feedback.
4. Paper-driven approval: 11 forms, 4 physical chops, 13 working days average versus a 5-day provincial target.
5. Only 38 of 310 service items (about 12%) can be initiated online; none can be completed fully online.
6. High frontline turnover (25%) with 3-month training cycles due to undocumented, scattered operational knowledge.
7. Provincial digital-government assessment ranking of 14/21 creating direct political pressure on leadership.

## 4. Focus Topics

- Cross-department data sharing and unified data standards
- One-form submission and composite-matter service redesign
- Fully-online service capability and citizen self-service
- Approval process re-engineering and electronic seals
- Knowledge management for frontline staff enablement
- Measurable alignment with provincial assessment indicators
